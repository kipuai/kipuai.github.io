## What's new in v2

1. **Approach — "Opportunity is co-discovered."** New section above *Why it exists*. Sharpens the "business leads, tech enables" frame into a co-discovery rhythm. Reason: a pure business-led intake produces backlogs full of "automate this report" and misses moves the business didn't know to ask for. We sit in the intersection and want the site to say so.
2. **Agents — folded into the KRI roadmap.** Rather than standing up Agent Operating Model as a parallel productized offering, we surface it as a typical *output* of the Readiness Index roadmap. Reason: keeps one front door (KRI), avoids competing offerings on the same page, and lets us productize AOM properly later once we have a delivered case study.
3. **Qualification language.** Dropped the "$50M+ in revenue" filter in favor of intent and executive sponsorship. Reason: a dollar threshold filters self-servingly and the LatAm mid-market sits well below US benchmarks anyway. What predicts a successful engagement is sponsorship and intent, not revenue.
4. **Timeline language.** Stripped specific week counts from KRI and roadmap-output descriptions; kept the phases. Reason: avoid committing to durations we can't yet stand behind across engagement types. Outcomes stay front and center; duration is a conversation on the intro call.
5. **Credibility band.** Logo marquee hidden until we have logos to show. The text credibility line stays, with the YC framing corrected (Jorge was a YC-backed founder, not an operator at YC).

Everything else below is v1 copy, untouched.

## Voice & tone

- **Confident, not braggy.** State the value plainly; let credentials do the boasting.
- **Editorial.** Short declarative sentences. Almost no marketing adjectives ("cutting-edge", "world-class", "innovative"). One or two strong verbs per paragraph.
- **Restrained.** When in doubt, cut a sentence.

# 2. Approach (the Kipu Readiness Index)

## 2.1 Hero

> # The Kipu Readiness Index.
> A structured diagnostic that tells you exactly where your organization stands on the path to becoming AI-native — and what to do next.

## 2.2 How we frame opportunity `[NEW in v2]`

> Sits above *Why it exists*. Establishes Kipu's point of view before the diagnostic gets introduced.

> ## Opportunity is co-discovered.
> AI value is realized in the business — but the best opportunities are not surfaced by the business alone. Pain lives in the line of business; what is newly possible lives with the people tracking the technology. A backlog built only from business intake fills with "automate this report." The transformational moves are the ones the business did not know to ask for.
>
> Kipu installs a recurring rhythm: business owners bring problems, technical leaders bring capability, and the intersection becomes the roadmap. Business owns outcomes. Tech owns capability literacy. We facilitate the conversation that makes both useful.

## 2.3 Why it exists

> ## Most AI strategies fail before they begin.
> Not because the technology isn't ready — but because the organization isn't. Data is fragmented. Talent is missing. Governance is unclear. Use cases are picked by enthusiasm, not leverage.
>
> The Kipu Readiness Index is the first step we take with every client. A focused engagement, a clear score, a prioritized roadmap. No fluff.

## 2.4 The five dimensions

> Visual: a five-point diagram (radar or sequential). Each dimension below gets ~2 sentences in the design.

> ### 1. Strategic Clarity
> Are AI investments tied to specific business outcomes, or driven by pressure to "do something with AI"?
>
> ### 2. Data Foundation
> Is your data accessible, governed, and trustworthy enough to power decisions and models?
>
> ### 3. Talent & Operating Model
> Do you have the roles, structures, and decision rights to execute? Or is AI sitting in one team, isolated from the business?
>
> ### 4. Use Case Portfolio
> Which use cases will move the P&L? Which are noise? We score them by impact, feasibility, and time to value.
>
> ### 5. Governance & Risk
> Are policies, controls, and accountability in place to scale AI responsibly?

## 2.5 What you get

> ## The deliverable.
> At the end of the engagement, you receive:
>
> - A **Kipu Readiness Score** across the five dimensions, benchmarked against peers.
> - A **prioritized 12-month roadmap** with sequenced initiatives, owners, and success metrics.
> - A **shortlist of three to five use cases** ranked by leverage and feasibility.
> - An **executive readout** for your board or leadership team.

## 2.6 How it works

> ## Three phases.
>
> **Discovery.** Stakeholder interviews, data audit, document review.
> **Diagnosis.** Scoring, peer benchmarking, use-case ranking.
> **Roadmap & Readout.** Sequenced 12-month plan, executive presentation.
>
> Engagement length is scoped on the intro call.

## 2.7 What the roadmap typically includes `[NEW in v2]`

> Sits beneath *How it works*. Replaces the standalone Agent Operating Model offering. Surfaces agent work as one of several roadmap outputs, without forcing it into the service taxonomy.

> ## What the roadmap usually contains.
> Every roadmap is shaped to the organization, but a few patterns recur.
>
> - **Use-case sequencing.** Which initiatives ship first, who owns them, and what success looks like at 90, 180, and 365 days.
> - **Data and platform groundwork.** The minimum platform investments required to unblock the prioritized use cases — not a multi-year data strategy.
> - **Capability and operating-model gaps.** Roles, decision rights, and enablement needed for the business to absorb what's being built.
> - **An agent operating model, when it applies.** For clients shipping their first agents, the roadmap typically includes ownership, access scoping, evals, and incident playbooks — sized to the stack, not a platform purchase.
>
> Execution is optional. You can run it yourself, with us, or with a partner we recommend.

## 2.8 FAQ

> **Who is this for?** Mid-market and enterprise organizations with serious intent to operate as AI-native and executive sponsorship from the CEO, COO, or CTO.
>
> **What does it cost?** Fixed scope, fixed fee. We share the number on the intro call.
>
> **What happens after?** Most clients move into one of two next steps: a Capability Building program for their teams, or an Implementation engagement on the top-ranked use case. You're under no obligation to do either.
